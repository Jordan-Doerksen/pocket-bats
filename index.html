Most people meet them as a shape in the dark. Up close they are soft, careful animals that hold the night together.

Three things people get wrong ↓

Three things people get wrong

They are blind.

No bat species is blind. All of them can see. Many fruit bats see very well in low light, and some species see ultraviolet.

They are flying mice.

They are not rodents. Bats are the only mammals that fly under their own power. Everything else that glides is falling with style.

They want your hair.

A bat near your head is hunting the insects near your head. It knows exactly where you are, which is the whole point of the next part.

See how it knows ↓

See with sound

A bat calls, then listens for the echo. From the echo it builds a picture of the dark: what is there, how far, and which way it is moving. It does this fast enough to catch a moth mid-turn.

The call is not a squeak
Most of it sits above human hearing. At close range some species are as loud as a smoke alarm, and you stand there hearing nothing at all.
Distance is just delay
Sound leaves, sound returns, and the wait between the two is the range. A later echo is a further wall. This is the whole trick.
The closing buzz
On the final approach the calls speed up into a blur, dozens in a single second, so the picture refreshes faster than the moth can dodge.
The moths answer back
Some hear the call coming and drop out of the air. Some tiger moths click back and jam the signal. This is an argument both sides have been having for millions of years.

Your turn. One call, one glimpse, and whatever you can remember.

What they do all night

1,400+

Known bat species. That is more than one in five mammal species on Earth.

Hundreds

Insects a small insect-eating bat can take in one night, mosquitoes included. A colony works through the number your imagination refuses to hold.

Upside down

Their foot tendons lock closed under their own weight. Hanging costs a bat no effort. Letting go is the part that takes a muscle.

Bananas

Also mangoes, guavas, and agave. Nectar bats pollinate over 500 plant species. Tequila exists because of a bat.

Two

Pups a year for many species, usually one. Bats are slow to replace their losses, which is why a lost colony stays lost.

Hang up for the day

Flying is expensive. Hanging is free. A bat's toes close under its own body weight, so the ceiling does the holding while the bat does the sleeping. The colony turns in before the light arrives.

Hanging costs nothing
The tendons in a bat's toes pull closed as the body pulls down. A sleeping bat holds on with no effort at all. A dead bat stays hanging.
The ceiling is the runway
Their wings cannot lift them from a standing start the way a bird's can. Hanging high means a takeoff is one letting-go and a fall into flight.
They turn themselves down
Many species drop their body temperature while they roost and idle through the day on almost nothing. Some sleep out an entire winter this way.
The same crack, for years
Bats come back to the roost they know: a cave, a hollow tree, a gap under a bridge. Seal that gap while they are out, and they arrive home to a wall.

Sunrise is not negotiable. Get them up.

The night shift

A bat does not snack. It works a shift. It leaves at dusk, eats its own body weight in insects across the night, and comes home before the light. Then it does that again tomorrow, and the tomorrow after that.

The mosquito arithmetic
A single small bat can take hundreds of insects an hour when the hunting is good. A colony over a field is the reason the field is bearable in July.
The farmer's unpaid staff
They eat the moths whose caterpillars eat the corn. Studies put the value of that work to agriculture in the billions of dollars a year, in the United States alone.
They catch with the tail
Not the mouth. The wing sweeps the insect into the skin stretched between the legs, and the bat curls forward to eat it, mid-air, without stopping.
Owls are the problem
Owls hunt in the same dark and fly almost silently, so a bat's ears get little warning. Hawks take the ones that leave the roost too early.

If you like them

Bats are having a hard century. A fungal disease called white-nose syndrome has killed millions of hibernating bats in North America since 2006. They raise one pup a year, so the losses do not grow back quickly.

Leave the roost alone
If bats are living in a building, the humane fix is a one-way exit after the pups can fly, never a sealed gap in the middle of summer.
Turn the light off
Floodlights on a barn or a tree line push bats off their commuting routes. Dark edges are habitat.
Let the yard be a little untidy
Night-flowering plants, a water source, and standing dead trees feed the insects that feed the bats.
